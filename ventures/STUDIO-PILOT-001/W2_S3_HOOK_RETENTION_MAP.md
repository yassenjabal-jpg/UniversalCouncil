## Retention design

The episode is mapped in **12 contiguous 50-second beats**. There is no interval longer than 50 seconds without a new reason to continue; the W2 hard ceiling is 90 seconds.

1. **0:00–0:50 — HOOK:** the employee appears American, but only the laptop may be in America.
2. **0:50–1:40 — SETUP:** a prosecuted Nashville case makes the mechanism physical and real.
3. **1:40–2:30 — REVEAL:** the laptop becomes a geographic disguise.
4. **2:30–3:20 — ESCALATION:** one workstation grows into distributed laptop farms and facilitators.
5. **3:20–4:10 — REVEAL:** 40+ homes, ~187 devices, 70+ companies—the scale lands.
6. **4:10–5:00 — RESET:** one “employee” breaks into seven separate trust signals.
7. **5:00–5:50 — ESCALATION:** identity, interview, device, location, work and payroll can be modular.
8. **5:50–6:40 — REVEAL:** stakes expand beyond wages, with strict allegation/proven-fact separation.
9. **6:40–7:30 — SETUP:** the physical U.S. presence starts becoming evidence.
10. **7:30–8:20 — REVEAL:** the disguise reverses into a trail investigators can follow.
11. **8:20–9:10 — PAYOFF:** the laptop itself is the worker's geographic stand-in—the title question is answered.
12. **9:10–10:00 — PAYOFF:** identity, device location, human location and account ownership are separate trust signals.

## Open-loop discipline

Seven loops are opened and closed deliberately. No material promise from the title or opening is held back artificially until the final seconds.

The major loops are:
- where the worker really is;
- why the physical U.S. laptop matters;
- why the infrastructure needs many devices;
- how one employee can split into multiple people/tools;
- why the scheme matters beyond salary fraud;
- how investigators can find it;
- what remote hiring was incorrectly treating as a single fact.
