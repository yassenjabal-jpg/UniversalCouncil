## Mechanism — evidence-supported version

1. An overseas worker uses a stolen, borrowed or fabricated identity to apply for remote work.
2. A U.S.-based facilitator provides a domestic address or receives employer equipment.
3. The employer sends a work laptop to that U.S. address.
4. The device remains physically inside the United States.
5. Remote-access software or KVM-over-IP hardware can allow an overseas operator to control the employer device.
6. To the employer, some location/network signals can therefore originate from a real U.S.-based machine even though the human operator is elsewhere.
7. Payroll, identity and account infrastructure may then be used to route proceeds or sustain the employment relationship.

This sequence must be written claim-by-claim. Do not imply every investigated team used every technique.

## Strongest proven mechanism example

The Matthew Isaac Knoot case is the cleanest explanatory spine because it reached sentencing.

DOJ says victim companies shipped laptops for an employee identity to Knoot's Nashville residences. Knoot then installed unauthorized remote-desktop applications; a North Korean IT worker operating from China could work through those machines while appearing to victim companies as if the employee were operating from Nashville.

This is the best candidate for the episode's first concrete reconstruction because it turns an abstract cyber scheme into a physical sequence the viewer can see.

## What changed in the threat

The FBI's October 13, 2026 alert says the model is evolving:

- more facilitators;
- smaller, more distributed laptop farms;
- remote desktop tools and KVM-over-IP hardware;
- use of established social/professional accounts;
- recruitment of third-party freelancers to apply, interview or even perform portions of the work;
- use of stolen personal information;
- extortion and data-theft risk in some cases.

This gives the episode a second act: the scheme is not merely “one North Korean pretends to be an American programmer.” It is a modular system in which identity, device, location, interviewing, work and payments can be split among different people.
