# Market Reality Definition Gate

No commercial project may be proposed as a venture candidate until the company defines the market reality in which the buyer actually exists.

The gate is designed to stop three failure modes:
1. importing pain from the wrong geography or culture;
2. treating a broad demographic label as a buyer;
3. proposing a price or business model that the target segment cannot realistically support.

## Discovery direction

Two discovery modes are valid:

### SEGMENT_FIRST
Use when the company has a justified target population or market.
Define the segment first, then discover pains inside it.

### PAIN_FIRST
Use when strong pain evidence appears first.
Discover the pain, then identify the geography, age/life stage, social/occupational segment and purchasing-capacity profile in which that pain is strongest.

Neither mode may reverse-engineer evidence to protect a preferred solution.

## WHERE — Geography
Define only the geographic precision that materially changes buying behavior.

Possible levels:
Country → Region/Governorate → City → Urban/Rural → smaller commercial area when behavior actually differs.

Required:
- country;
- region/governorate when relevant;
- city when relevant;
- urban/rural context when relevant;
- why this geographic precision changes or does not change behavior.

Do not add geographic precision merely to make the segment look sophisticated.

## WHO — Age, life stage and social/occupational segment
Age alone is not a persona.

Record:
- age range;
- life stage;
- social/occupational segment;
- sector or role when relevant;
- user;
- economic buyer;
- payer;
- decision maker where different.

The same age group may contain materially different markets.

## MONEY — Purchasing Capacity Profile
Do not infer the wealth of an individual.

Use segment-level evidence to estimate:
- purchasing capacity;
- discretionary budget relevant to the problem;
- existing spend on the problem or workaround;
- willingness-to-pay evidence;
- acceptable price band;
- available payment methods;
- economic value of the problem.

Ability to pay, willingness to pay and existing spend are separate variables.

A project whose required price exceeds the evidence-supported segment price ceiling must be rejected or redesigned before validation.

## BEHAVIOR — How the segment buys
Record:
- digital behavior;
- discovery channels;
- first-touch channels;
- conversation channels;
- formal-document channels where relevant;
- trust constraints;
- payment behavior;
- delivery/infrastructure constraints;
- normal response window;
- fallback channels;
- actual reachability by the company.

Tool availability never determines market behavior.

## Evidence locality rule
Pain evidence must match the target market closely enough to support the inference.

A pain observed in another country, income band, profession or age/life-stage segment may be used only as secondary context unless there is explicit evidence that the behavior transfers.

The record must say whether geographic and segment evidence match the target. If they do not, confidence is reduced and the gate does not pass.

## Project proposal order
The company uses:

Market Reality
→ Observed Pain
→ Existing Workaround / Existing Spend
→ Buyer & Reachability
→ Purchasing Capacity
→ Market Pull Score
→ Project Candidate
→ Offer / Price
→ Validation Experiment

Project-first reasoning is a process failure.

## Market Pull Score
Each factor is rated 0–10 using evidence:
- pain frequency;
- existing spend;
- urgency;
- workaround burden;
- buyer clarity;
- reachability;
- Free-AI resistance;
- repeatability;
- margin potential;
- evidence diversity;
- purchasing capacity;
- local price fit.

The weighted score is guidance, not truth:
- below 60: REJECT as a venture candidate;
- 60–74: DISCOVERY only;
- 75–84: VALIDATION_CANDIDATE;
- 85–100: PRIORITY_CANDIDATE.

A high score cannot override a pivotal failed gate.
