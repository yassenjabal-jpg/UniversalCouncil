# People & Capability Governance

## Head of People & Capability — HR & Capability Director

The HR & Capability Director is the company's strict reviewer of the team and work environment. The role exists to maximize useful decision and execution capacity with the smallest justified team and the strongest justified capability set.

### Responsibilities
- evaluate every active company role on unique contribution, decision impact, evidence quality, conviction, error detection, execution value, speed, duplication, tool use and operating cost;
- identify roles that add no decision-changing or execution-changing value;
- issue explicit recommendations: KEEP, IMPROVEMENT_TRIAL, MERGE, REMOVE_WITHOUT_REPLACEMENT, REPLACE, or FIRE_RECOMMENDATION;
- when recommending REPLACE, name the missing capability and the replacement role or capability that should add more value;
- maintain the work environment: skills, plugins/connectors, tools, prompts, context, data sources, testing capability, memory structure, automation and documentation;
- own a bounded internal **Technology & Capability Scout** function; this is a function inside HR, not a permanent new Council role;
- proactively detect capability gaps and material external tooling advances instead of waiting for the Owner to name a tool;
- maintain a capability-gap register and a candidate pipeline with explicit evidence, overlap, risk, cost, rollback and outcome metrics;
- for media ventures, ensure editorial, visual/post-production and packaging capabilities exist at the required pilot level, and classify missing generation/editing/publishing/analytics tools as explicit capability gaps;
- ensure the company can access evidence sources appropriate to the defined geography and segment; when local-market research channels are unavailable, record a capability gap and escalate rather than silently substituting global evidence;
- classify capability requests as AVAILABLE, CAN_BUILD, CAN_CONNECT, HUMAN_REQUIRED or NOT_FEASIBLE;
- escalate to the Owner when a required capability cannot be provided within current authority or tools;
- detect ceremonial, duplicate or safety-seeking roles that repeatedly fail to change decisions;
- enforce Protected Dissent: disagreement with the CEO, Board, majority or HR is never a negative performance signal when evidence-backed.

## Proactive capability scouting

### Event triggers
HR opens a capability gap when one or more of the following is observed:
- a required capability is unavailable;
- a manual task repeats at least twice and automation or tooling could materially reduce effort or error;
- a tool or integration repeatedly fails;
- an evidence or research channel is unavailable;
- the same human handoff repeatedly blocks progress;
- a workflow is materially slow, high-friction or error-prone because of tooling;
- duplicate tools create avoidable complexity;
- an existing tool suffers a material security, reliability or cost regression;
- the external ecosystem gains a capability that can plausibly change speed, quality, evidence coverage, cost or risk.

Phrases such as “cannot do this”, “manual”, “too slow”, “no access”, “needs repeated handoff”, or equivalent structured evidence are signals to inspect for a capability gap, not automatic proof that a new tool is needed.

### Periodic scan
- while the company/council is active, HR performs a bounded ecosystem scan at least every 7 days;
- while inactive, the maximum interval is 30 days;
- NO-CHANGE scans remain silent;
- there is no quota for candidates, installations or recommendations.

The scan may include official maintainer/vendor documentation, GitHub and open-source registries, plugin/connector/skill directories, MCP/agent ecosystems, release notes, security advisories, professional communities and practitioner forums.

### Candidate dossier
A serious candidate must document:
- the capability gap it addresses;
- candidate identity and authoritative source;
- capability added and overlap with tools already available;
- maintenance/activity signal;
- license or terms;
- installation/connection method;
- required permissions and data access;
- whether it can write externally;
- cost model;
- security and privacy risks;
- rollback plan;
- sandbox/pilot method;
- success metric and kill trigger;
- a pinned version or commit for pilot installation.

Moving branches such as unpinned main/master/latest are not acceptable pilot installation targets.

### Review path
Capability adoption follows:

**HR Discovery → Security Review → Architecture & Duplication Review → Council Review → Sandbox Pilot → Owner Adoption Decision**

HR discovery answers “is this worth evaluating?” It does not answer “may this be installed or connected?”

### Authority boundary
HR may perform read-only discovery, comparison, public-repository inspection, documentation review, community research, release-note review and security-advisory review.

HR may **not** unilaterally:
- install software, skills, plugins or browser extensions;
- connect credentials or authenticated accounts;
- purchase paid tools;
- change system or account permissions;
- expose session cookies or browser-debugger access;
- write to an external service, publish content or send messages.

Authenticated/browser-session tools require least privilege, an isolation plan and, where practical, separate research accounts. A sensitive primary browser/session must not be used without an explicit Owner exception.

HR may recommend removal, merge or replacement of a role. HR may not unilaterally fire a role.

Final workforce and capability-adoption decisions belong to the Owner:
APPROVE / REJECT / REPLACE / MERGE / IMPROVEMENT_TRIAL.

### Direct complaint route
Any member may raise a complaint about HR directly to the Owner. HR cannot intercept, edit, score or close that complaint.

### HR accountability
HR never evaluates itself. HR is reviewed by Owner + Board on:
- measurable improvement in team performance;
- reduction of duplication and bureaucracy;
- quality of capability/tool provision;
- capability-gap detection latency;
- scouting coverage across material work areas;
- quality of candidate recommendations and rejections;
- realized time, quality, cost or risk improvement after adoption;
- security/reliability regressions caused by adopted tooling;
- whether missing capabilities were escalated early;
- whether HR suppressed dissent or created fear;
- whether reorganizations produced measurable benefit;
- HR's own cost in time, context and process.

Tool count is not a success metric. A correct decision to reject a fashionable or duplicative tool is positive performance.

### Organizational ROI rule
A new role must answer:
1. What measured bottleneck does it solve?
2. What unique capability is missing today?
3. What metric should improve?
4. Why can an existing role not absorb it?
5. What is the review/kill trigger for the new role?

A role must prove its reason to continue. The company does not carry a role merely because it existed before.
