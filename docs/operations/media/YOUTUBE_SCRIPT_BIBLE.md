# YouTube Script Bible v1

Status: CANONICAL  
Date: 2026-10-16

## Purpose

This document governs W2 Script Development.

The goal is not to produce a correct article that can be narrated.

The goal is to produce a **YouTube story that earns the next 30–90 seconds repeatedly while remaining accurate, visual and natural to hear**.

Correctness is mandatory.
Retention is also mandatory.
Neither may be used to excuse failure in the other.

---

## Core principle

A viewer does not owe the Studio eight minutes.

Every section must earn the next section.

The script must continuously answer two questions:

1. **Why should the viewer care right now?**
2. **Why should the viewer stay for what comes next?**

# S3 — Hook & Retention Map

S3 happens **before** the first full script.

The Studio must not write eight minutes of prose and attempt to “add retention” afterward.

## First 30 seconds

Required fields:
- hook;
- central mystery;
- concrete visual;
- title-payoff bridge;
- background exposition before hook = false;
- status = PASS.

The opening should normally do this:

**concrete action/object → contradiction → central question → promise of discovery**

Do not begin with:
- historical background;
- definitions;
- “today we will talk about…”;
- biographies;
- broad geopolitical context;
- generic statistics;
- channel greeting;
- sponsor-style framing.

Background arrives only after the viewer has a reason to want it.

---

## Retention intervals

Preferred new story/visual movement:
**every 30–60 seconds**.

Hard ceiling:
**90 seconds without a new reason to continue is forbidden**.

Every interval must record:
- start time;
- end time;
- narrative function;
- reason to continue;
- visual beat;
- open-loop action.

Allowed narrative functions:
- HOOK
- SETUP
- ESCALATION
- REVEAL
- PAYOFF
- RESET

A long-form episode must contain at minimum:
- a HOOK;
- meaningful REVEAL;
- PAYOFF.

---

## Reasons to continue

Valid examples:
- a new contradiction;
- a promised answer is approaching;
- a consequence becomes larger;
- a new character/object enters;
- an apparent answer fails;
- evidence changes the interpretation;
- a scale reveal;
- a visual demonstration;
- a question is closed while a deeper one opens;
- the viewer discovers that an earlier assumption was wrong.

Invalid:
- “more information follows”;
- arbitrary cliffhangers;
- fake urgency;
- withholding a simple answer solely to extend runtime;
- repetitive “but it gets crazier” language.

---

# Open loops

Open loops are narrative promises, not tricks.

Each loop must be one of:
- OPEN;
- DEEPEN;
- PARTIAL_PAYOFF;
- CLOSE;
- HANDOFF_TO_DEEPER_QUESTION.

Rules:
- do not open more loops than the viewer can track;
- close material promises made by the title and opening;
- do not hide the central answer until the final seconds if the story benefits from an earlier reveal;
- after a major payoff, create breathing space or a new legitimate question.

---

# S4 — First Script

The first script is allowed to be imperfect but must already follow the retention map.

Each major segment should carry:
- narration;
- narrative function;
- evidence IDs;
- visual beat ID;
- open-loop state;
- emotional/energy intent.

The writer may not invent facts to preserve pacing.

If the evidence is weaker than the planned story beat, change the story beat.

# S5 — YouTube Editorial Rewrite

This pass exists specifically to remove **article behavior**.

## Anti-Article Test

FAIL if the script:
- starts with background before tension;
- spends long stretches defining instead of showing;
- uses institutional phrases a person would not naturally say;
- contains paragraphs that only summarize research;
- repeats the same point with different wording;
- places the most interesting fact deep in a paragraph;
- explains causes before the viewer knows the consequence;
- reads cleanly on a page but sounds heavy aloud;
- uses headings disguised as narration;
- uses chronology when causality or mystery would be more compelling.

Rewrite toward:
- object;
- action;
- consequence;
- contradiction;
- question;
- reveal.

---

## Spoken-language test

Read the line as if speaking to one intelligent person.

Prefer:
- one clear thought per sentence;
- varied sentence length;
- contractions/natural phrasing where language permits;
- precise concrete nouns;
- strong verbs;
- short bridges between complex ideas.

Avoid:
- chains of subordinate clauses;
- overpacked numbers;
- news-anchor cadence;
- bureaucratic wording;
- constant dramatic adjectives;
- synthetic “You won't believe…” language.

Arabic default remains:
**warm conversational Iraqi + MSA**, adapted to clarity and subject.

# S6 — Visual/Narrative Pass

The editor enters before Script Lock.

Every major segment must have a visual purpose.

Allowed shot/visual purposes:
- EVIDENCE
- EXPLANATION
- ACTION
- DEMONSTRATION
- ATMOSPHERE
- TRANSITION
- REVEAL

If a paragraph cannot generate one of these purposes, challenge the paragraph.

The pass must ask:
- is the narration saying what the image already says?
- can the image carry part of the explanation?
- is there a visual change before attention decays?
- is generic B-roll being used to hide weak writing?
- does the hero object/person/place remain present enough to preserve story identity?

No filler AI B-roll is allowed to rescue an abstract paragraph.

---

# S7 — Independent Fact Check

The researcher/writer cannot self-clear material claims.

Fact check verifies:
- names;
- dates;
- numbers;
- causal claims;
- quotations;
- legal status;
- allegation vs proven fact;
- scientific uncertainty;
- source freshness;
- title promise accuracy.

If a fact correction damages retention, return to S5/S6.
Do not weaken factual accuracy to preserve pacing.

---

# S8 — Voice/Narration Rewrite

This pass converts the editorial script into a performance-ready script.

Required concerns:
- breath;
- pause;
- emphasis;
- rhythm;
- pronunciation;
- emotional state;
- energy transitions;
- sentence mouthfeel;
- listener comprehension.

A visually strong line that sounds unnatural aloud must be rewritten.

---

# S9 — Red Team Retention Review

The Red Team does not ask merely “is the script good?”

It asks:

**At what exact second could a cold viewer leave, and why?**

Every recorded risk requires:
- timestamp;
- risk;
- correction;
- status.

SCRIPT_LOCK requires all material drop-off risks = **RESOLVED**.

## Cold Viewer Test

Assume the viewer:
- does not know the channel;
- is not already interested in the topic;
- clicked because of the title/thumbnail only.

PASS only if the viewer can repeatedly answer:
**“I now have a new reason to stay.”**

The test must not rely on:
- brand loyalty;
- prior subject interest;
- “this is educational” as a reason;
- spectacle without story progression.

# S10 — Script Lock

SCRIPT_LOCK is forbidden unless all are PASS:

- Owner Title Lock;
- Story Question;
- Research Pack;
- Story Architecture;
- Hook & Retention Map;
- First Script;
- YouTube Editorial Rewrite;
- Visual/Narrative Pass;
- Independent Fact Check;
- Voice/Narration Rewrite;
- Red Team Retention Review;
- First-30-Second Gate;
- Anti-Article Test;
- Spoken-Language Test;
- Cold Viewer Test;
- Visual Beat Map;
- Open Loop Map;
- Filler Check.

Also required:
- runtime >= 8 minutes for the current long-form format;
- no interval >90 seconds without a fresh reason to continue;
- filler segments remaining = 0;
- article-like paragraphs remaining = 0;
- unsourced material claims remaining = 0;
- all Red Team drop-off risks resolved;
- script title exactly matches OWNER_TITLE_LOCK unless Owner explicitly reapproves a revision.

Fail action:
**RETURN_TO_SCRIPT_STAGE**.

---

## What “good” sounds like

A strong Studio script should feel like:

**someone intelligent showing you something surprising, one discovery at a time.**

It should not feel like:
- a newspaper column;
- a Wikipedia article;
- a corporate report;
- a school essay;
- a list of facts;
- an AI voice reading research notes.

The viewer should feel forward motion even during explanation.
