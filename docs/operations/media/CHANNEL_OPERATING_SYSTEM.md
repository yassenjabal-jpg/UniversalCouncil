## Channel promise

The channel is broad in topic but narrow in editorial promise:

**Reveal the hidden logic behind something familiar, strange, important, or easy to overlook.**

## Master project architecture

Every episode moves through:

**Writing → Pre-production → Production**

No phase may be skipped.

A later phase may return work upstream only through a formal return ticket.

## Phase 1 — Writing

### Title development comes first

The Studio searches for **stories**, not merely timely subjects.

It reads:
- YouTube demand and outliers;
- current market interest;
- social/platform trends;
- specialist/community signals;
- durable audience questions.

Before the Owner sees anything, each candidate must become a YouTube-native story premise with:
- one concrete hero anchor;
- one contradiction, mystery or unresolved tension;
- explicit viewer stakes;
- one key visual;
- one thumbnail hypothesis;
- exactly two title variants.

The Studio produces **exactly three story candidates using three distinct story archetypes**.

Each title is judged together with its thumbnail hypothesis and must pass:
- Stranger Click Test;
- YouTube Title Style check;
- promise accuracy.

If YouTube-native discovery is materially degraded, the workflow enters **DEGRADED_DISCOVERY_HOLD** rather than replacing platform evidence with generic news relevance.

The Owner chooses one title from one story.

That creates:
**OWNER_TITLE_LOCK**

The script cannot start before this gate.


### Script development

W2 writes for a viewer, not for a reader.

S0 Story Question
S1 Research Pack
S2 Story Architecture
S3 Hook & Retention Map
S4 First Script
S5 YouTube Editorial Rewrite
S6 Visual/Narrative Pass
S7 Independent Fact Check
S8 Voice/Narration Rewrite
S9 Red Team Retention Review
S10 Locked Script

Before Script Lock the episode must prove:
- a concrete hook before background exposition;
- a strong first 30 seconds;
- a reason to continue at least every 90 seconds;
- preferred visual/story change every 30–60 seconds;
- managed open loops;
- a visual beat map;
- no unresolved filler;
- no article-like paragraphs;
- spoken-language clarity;
- Cold Viewer PASS;
- Red Team drop-off risks resolved.

Detailed rules:
`YOUTUBE_SCRIPT_BIBLE.md`.


Exit:
**SCRIPT_LOCK**

## Phase 2 — Pre-production

Starts only after Script Lock.

Sequence:
1. script breakdown;
2. Visual Story Case;
3. art direction;
4. asset plan;
5. rights/source preclearance;
6. shot design;
7. storyboard;
8. camera/continuity plan;
9. sonic concept;
10. narration plan;
11. title-promise alignment;
12. animatic;
13. Production Readiness Review.

The early sonic concept exists so sound influences picture planning.

Final sound design still waits for Picture Lock.

The early title strategy exists in Writing.

Final thumbnail/packaging craft remains late in Production.

Exit:
**PRODUCTION_READY_LOCK**

## Phase 3 — Production

Production is a serial manufacturing line.

One craft department active at a time.

One primary program per stage.

No downstream silent repair of upstream locked work.

Department sequence:

1. Voice & Narration
2. Visual Asset Production
3. Motion & Compositing
4. Picture Editing
5. Sound Design & Mix
6. Thumbnail & Packaging
7. Assurance & Delivery
8. Learning & Archive

See `STUDIO_MASTER_PIPELINE.md` for the exact software assigned to every stage.

## Title authority

The selected title is controlled by the Owner.

After Owner Title Lock:
- the Studio may propose a revision;
- it must show evidence and downstream impact;
- no automatic title change is allowed;
- Owner reapproval is mandatory.

## Production feedback gates

Every department ends with a Handoff Package.

The next department either:
- **ACCEPTS**, then starts; or
- **REJECTS**, then creates a Return Ticket to the source department.

A downstream department may not repair a locked upstream artifact on its own.

## Specialized-tool-first rule

Professional craft uses specialized creative software.

Code is appropriate for:
- orchestration;
- manifests;
- checkpoints;
- validation;
- QC;
- provenance;
- metrics.

Code is not the default replacement for:
- editing;
- motion design;
- sound design;
- mixing;
- visual design;
- finishing.
