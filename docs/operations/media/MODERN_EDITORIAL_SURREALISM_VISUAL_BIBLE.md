# Modern Editorial Surrealism — Channel Visual Identity Bible

**Status:** Candidate rebuilt identity for Owner review  
**Decision date:** 2026-10-14  
**Primary reference:** user-provided reference video `1000061003.mp4`  
**Supersedes:** `Editorial Renaissance Motion` as the proposed channel visual system  
**Scope:** thumbnails, key art, episode frames, motion grammar, visual packaging, generated/archival asset treatment

---

## 1. Source of truth

For this identity rebuild, the reference video is the primary visual authority.

The previous architecture-heavy experiments are **not** source material for the new identity. Greek, Babylonian, Baghdadi, classical architectural, marble, lapis, and ornamental motifs may appear only when an episode's subject genuinely calls for them.

The brand must be recognized by **design grammar**, not by repeating the same scenery.

### Reference observations from the video

Representative moments:

- **~1s:** saturated cobalt/azure field, one classical human figure, a theatrical curtain, large negative space, elegant editorial headline.
- **~4s:** close object/process shot on a nearly white field; botanical grafting becomes the visual story.
- **~7s:** classical/Renaissance-styled portrait holding a pear; clean white background; the object is the conceptual hook.
- **~10s:** graphic pear/object treatment against blue; halftone/print-like treatment and modern editorial annotation.
- **~13s:** warm ivory editorial spread; framed conceptual image on one side, serif headline and restrained body copy on the other.

The identity therefore does **not** depend on architecture. It depends on contrast between eras, strong editorial composition, conceptual objects, controlled surrealism, and clean motion.

- history

## 3. Core visual thesis

> **One clear idea, expressed through one memorable visual metaphor, staged like a premium editorial spread.**

Every frame should feel designed, not decorated.

The visual identity is built from:
1. **Editorial clarity**
2. **Strong negative space**
3. **One hero object or figure**
4. **Unexpected but understandable visual metaphor**
5. **Contrast between old and new**
6. **Controlled color fields**
7. **Elegant typography**
8. **Meaning-driven motion**
9. **Episode-level visual uniqueness**

---

## 4. Brand personality

The channel should feel:

- intelligent
- curious
- cultured
- contemporary
- confident
- calm
- slightly strange
- visually literate
- premium without being luxurious for its own sake
- playful without becoming childish
- historical when needed, but never museum-like by default

Avoid:
- “ancient civilization channel” appearance
- news-channel graphics
- generic AI cinematic fantasy
- cyberpunk/futurist UI
- constant gold/marble
- tourism-poster composition
- ornate historical collage as a default
- fixed arches/columns/curtains repeated across episodes

## 5. Color system

### 5.1 Brand anchors

The reference video supports a small set of strong anchors:

- **Electric Cobalt / Azure** — primary field color  
  Approx. reference range: `#0F57AE` to `#1B79C9`
- **Paper Ivory** — primary light field  
  Approx. `#F2EFE8`
- **Ink Charcoal** — text/contrast  
  Approx. `#1D1B1A`

These three create recognition, but **must not appear in the same proportion every episode**.

### 5.2 Episode accents

Each episode receives **one subject-driven accent**, for example:

- pear / nature: muted pear green
- economics: brass, copper, warm ochre, muted banknote green
- technology: cool silver, acid yellow, cyan accent
- science: mineral red, botanical green, ultraviolet accent
- history: burgundy, parchment tan, oxidized green
- social/cultural: coral, plum, rust, soft violet

Rule:
> **Brand anchor + one episode accent.**

Do not force blue into every thumbnail. Some episodes may be primarily ivory, green, burgundy, or another controlled field while still retaining the overall grammar.

---

## 6. Typography

Typography is editorial, not decorative.

### Arabic
Use:
- a refined Arabic display face with strong shape recognition for headlines
- a clean readable Arabic text face for micro-copy or diagrams
- compact line count
- generous spacing around the headline

Thumbnail rule:
- usually **2–4 words**
- one primary text block maximum
- no explanatory subtitle unless essential
- no ornate calligraphy as a default

### Latin / numbers
Use high-contrast editorial serif or disciplined modern serif for:
- dates
- numbers
- English labels
- citations
- occasional editorial contrast

Typography should behave like a magazine spread, not a social-media sticker.

---

## 7. Composition grammar

### Mandatory principles

- **35–60% negative space** in most key frames
- **one dominant hero object/figure**
- asymmetrical balance preferred
- one visual idea per frame
- the subject may break the grid intentionally
- background can be a flat color field, clean paper field, or grounded real environment
- detail is concentrated around the hero, not everywhere

### Allowed layout families

The channel should rotate between at least four composition families:

**L1 — Hero on Field**  
One figure/object on a strong color field, headline opposite or integrated into the negative space.

**L2 — Object Macro**  
A close object/process becomes the entire story; minimal text.

**L3 — Editorial Split**  
Conceptual image/artifact on one side, headline or compact explanation on the other.

**L4 — Surreal Stage**  
A physically impossible but understandable scene: scale shift, impossible object relationship, old/new collision, transformed material, or visual contradiction.

Do not reuse the same layout family in consecutive thumbnails unless the topic demands it.

## 8. Image language

Preferred sources:

- classical/Renaissance figures as cutouts when relevant
- archival photographs
- modern photography
- botanical/scientific objects
- tools and mechanisms
- consumer objects
- diagrams
- documents
- maps
- paintings and engravings
- clean studio-like object photography
- AI-generated conceptual imagery when clearly treated as illustration/metaphor

### Classical art rule

Classical figures are **one tool**, not the channel subject.

They can create:
- contrast between historical and contemporary
- humor
- cultural tension
- scale
- human presence

They must not appear in every episode.

### Architecture rule

Architecture is **topic-dependent only**.

It may appear:
- when the episode is about a city, building, civilization, public space, infrastructure, or spatial system
- as a subtle framing device when conceptually justified

It must not function as the brand's permanent border or default thumbnail template.

---

## 9. Visual metaphor system

Every episode must define **one visual metaphor before production**.

Examples:

- inflation → a familiar object physically expanding while value markers shrink
- banking → money moving through an unexpected visible mechanism rather than a generic vault
- time → a shadow, fruit, machine, or human routine transformed into a time system
- Baghdad → a circular-plan logic emerging from a real object or diagram rather than a fantasy city render
- AI → an ordinary human artifact partially completed by an invisible system
- memory → objects losing detail while labels remain sharp

A good metaphor:
- is readable in under one second
- is specific to the episode
- can drive both thumbnail and motion language
- cannot be reused unchanged for another topic

---

## 10. Motion grammar

The reference video suggests **designed transitions**, not generic edits.

Preferred motion:

- curtain reveal
- object wipe
- page/field shift
- cutout entrance
- scale transformation
- object-to-diagram transformation
- mask reveal
- foreground occlusion
- print/halftone build
- botanical growth
- assembly/disassembly
- match cut by silhouette
- color-field reset
- type appearing after the viewer has seen the object

Motion rule:
> **The object causes the transition whenever possible.**

Avoid:
- random whip pans
- glitch
- preset transition packs
- camera movement with no semantic reason
- animating every element simultaneously

---

## 11. Texture and material

The new identity is cleaner than the previous architecture direction.

Allowed:
- subtle paper grain
- halftone
- print imperfections
- light film texture
- fabric
- botanical texture
- real object surface
- archival marks where relevant

Use sparingly:
- marble
- gold
- stone
- ornamental tile
- ancient motifs

Forbidden as defaults:
- fake parchment everywhere
- excessive cracks
- museum-display gold
- heavy historical distress
- ornate frames on every image

## 12. Thumbnail system

A channel thumbnail must be recognizable as part of the same family **without looking like the previous thumbnail**.

### Fixed brand rules
- one strong hero
- clear negative space
- editorial typography
- disciplined palette
- one conceptual twist
- mobile readability
- no filler detail

### Mandatory episode differences
Each new thumbnail must change at least **four** of these:
- hero object
- layout family
- camera/crop
- dominant field color
- accent color
- text position
- image source type
- lighting direction
- texture treatment
- visual metaphor

### Anti-template rule
> No more than two major structural devices may carry over from the immediately previous thumbnail.

Examples of a major structural device:
- right-side hero / left-side text
- blue full-field background
- large arch
- curtain
- circular object
- gold object
- identical headline placement

### Packaging workflow
For each episode:
1. write five title hypotheses
2. create three visually distinct thumbnail hypotheses
3. test at phone size
4. reject the most generic option even if it is beautiful
5. keep only the option with the strongest click promise and clearest metaphor

---

## 13. Episode uniqueness contract

Before production, every episode must define:

- **1 hero object**
- **1 visual metaphor**
- **1 dominant layout family**
- **1 episode accent color**
- **3 episode-specific motifs**
- **1 recurring diagram language**
- **1 unique transition motif**

These are not automatically reused in the next episode.

---

## 14. Evidence vs illustration

The channel is documentary.

Therefore:
- archive and real evidence must be distinguishable from illustration
- generated historical-looking imagery must not be presented as real archival evidence
- diagrams and conceptual compositions are explanation tools
- synthetic realism requires disclosure review when a viewer could mistake it for documentary footage
- visual beauty never substitutes for evidence

---

## 15. Red Team rejection test

Reject a frame or thumbnail if any of the following is true:

1. It could be pasted unchanged into another episode.
2. Its main value is merely “beautiful.”
3. It repeats the last episode's composition.
4. It uses architecture or classical art without a narrative reason.
5. It looks like a generic AI museum/fantasy image.
6. The visual metaphor is unclear.
7. The hero object is not readable at mobile size.
8. The image has more than one competing focal point.
9. The text explains what the image should already communicate.
10. It depends on blue/gold/marble rather than an idea.

---

## 16. Council review synthesis

### Visual Art Director / Design
The reference's strongest asset is not historical decoration; it is **editorial staging plus conceptual juxtaposition**.

### Media
The system must work across economics, science, technology, history, and culture. Topic-specific imagery must therefore remain flexible.

### Human
Recognition at thumbnail size depends on silhouette, contrast, and one dominant idea.

### Audience/Packaging
A beautiful frame is not enough. The thumbnail must create a clear curiosity gap.

### Execution
The system is practical for Photoshop/Illustrator/After Effects workflows because it relies on cutouts, color fields, masks, diagrams, and controlled compositing rather than massive 3D environment production.

### Risk
Synthetic imagery must never impersonate real documentary evidence.

### Customer Skeptic
The identity must help a viewer recognize quality quickly without making every upload look the same.

### Moat Auditor
The defensible part of the identity is the repeatable **metaphor + editorial composition + motion grammar**, not a fixed palette or ornament set that competitors can copy in one prompt.

### Red Team
The previous architecture-heavy direction fails because it turns identity into a template. This rebuild removes that dependency.

---

## 17. Acceptance test

A finished episode passes the identity gate only if:

- [ ] the hero object is unique to the episode
- [ ] the visual metaphor is understandable
- [ ] the composition contains meaningful negative space
- [ ] the palette uses one subject-driven accent
- [ ] the frame can be identified as channel work without depending on a repeated arch/column/curtain
- [ ] the thumbnail remains legible at phone size
- [ ] the episode has at least three unique visual motifs
- [ ] motion follows meaning
- [ ] archival evidence and illustration are clearly separated
- [ ] Red Team cannot reasonably label it a repeated template

---

## 18. Final identity statement

> **Modern Editorial Surrealism turns documentary ideas into elegant visual metaphors: clean editorial fields, one memorable object or figure, controlled collision of past and present, bold negative space, restrained typography, and motion generated by meaning rather than presets.**

The identity should feel coherent across the channel while allowing every episode to look unmistakably like a different story.

## 19. Council-authored generation brief gate

No image-generation or AI-video-generation tool may receive an improvised prompt.

Before execution, the Council must produce a versioned artifact using:
`docs/templates/media/VISUAL_GENERATION_BRIEF.md`

The **Visual Prompt / Generation Brief Compiler** is a composed capability, not a permanent role.

Composed by:
- Visual Art Director / Frame Curator;
- Thumbnail & Packaging Designer;
- Synthesis & Alternatives Designer.

Independent approval:
- Red Team / Anti-Slop.

Execution:
- Technical Operator only.

Hard rules:

> **NO PROMPT, NO GENERATION.**  
> **NO ANTI-SLOP APPROVAL, NO EXECUTION.**

The approved brief is the execution contract. The Technical Operator and generation tool may not materially rewrite, embellish, simplify, or reinterpret it. If the tool cannot execute the brief faithfully, the task returns to the Council.

Every brief must explicitly contain:
- one-sentence visual metaphor;
- hero object;
- image language;
- composition and negative-space target;
- camera/crop;
- background;
- brand anchors + episode accent;
- lighting and texture;
- typography direction;
- allowed elements;
- forbidden elements;
- difference from previous thumbnail;
- reference-fidelity notes;
- mobile-legibility requirements;
- Anti-AI-Slop constraints;
- documentary evidence boundary;
- complete final generation instruction;
- negative/rejection constraints;
- prompt version;
- Anti-Slop approval status.

For each set of three thumbnail hypotheses, use materially different image languages:
1. Object Macro;
2. Editorial Cutout;
3. Conceptual Surrealism.

At least one of each three hypotheses must contain **no architecture at all**, unless architecture is inseparable from the episode subject.
