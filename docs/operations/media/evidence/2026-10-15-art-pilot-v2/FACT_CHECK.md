## Claim ledger

### C1 — Ancient Egyptian daily cycle used 12 daytime + 12 nighttime hours
**Disposition:** PASS

The Metropolitan Museum of Art states that the daily cycle was divided into twenty-four hours: twelve of the day and twelve of the night.

Source:
https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/telling-time-in-ancient-egypt

### C2 — Night hours were associated with decan star groups
**Disposition:** PASS

The Met describes the nighttime hours as apparently reckoned using groups of stars called decans. Museo Egizio describes diagonal star tables arranged to record the twelve hours of night through decan sequences.

Sources:
https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/telling-time-in-ancient-egypt
https://www.museoegizio.it/en/discover/funded-projects/the-diagonal-star-tables-of-ancient-egypt-corpus-texts-and-cosmology

### C3 — Seasonal hours were unequal
**Disposition:** PASS

Cambridge University Press excerpt from *Time: From Earth Rotation to Atomic Physics* states that day and night varied through the year and hour lengths differed accordingly.

Source:
https://assets.cambridge.org/97811071/97282/excerpt/9781107197282_excerpt.pdf

### C4 — Equal constant equinoctial hours were used for astronomical purposes; Hipparchus is commonly associated with early adoption
**Disposition:** PASS WITH CAREFUL WORDING

The Cambridge excerpt says seasonal hours were replaced for astronomical purposes by 24 equal constant parts known as equinoctial hours, and that Hipparchus was apparently the first to adopt them in place of unequal seasonal hours.

Required narration wording:
“ويُنسب إلى هيبارخوس تبنّي هذا النظام مبكرًا”
—not “هيبارخوس اخترع 24 ساعة.”

Source:
https://assets.cambridge.org/97811071/97282/excerpt/9781107197282_excerpt.pdf

## Evidence boundary

The Met object used on screen remains authentic Public Domain/Open Access evidence. Generated images, hand imagery, diagrams and motion graphics are explanatory/illustrative only and must never be presented as archaeological evidence.
