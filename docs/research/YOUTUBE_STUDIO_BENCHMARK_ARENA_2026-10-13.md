# Universal Council — YouTube Studio Benchmark Arena
Date: 2026-10-13
Status: FINAL BENCHMARK + ADOPTION PASS

## Purpose

Compare the Media Venture against high-performing YouTube/video studios that produce researched, visually sophisticated explainers and documentaries.

This is not a style-copy exercise. The goal is to identify professional production patterns that repeatedly appear in successful studios and adopt only practices that improve editorial quality, factual reliability, visual storytelling, packaging, production control, review quality, audio/visual craft and repeatability.

## Benchmarked studios / teams

### Kurzgesagt
Public process:
Research → Script → Illustration → Voiceover/Animation → Music/SFX.

Observed practices:
- deep research using books and primary sources;
- expert consultation;
- source sheet for claims;
- extensive script iteration before lock;
- visual metaphors explored before final illustration;
- voiceover provides timing for animation;
- music and SFX treated as dedicated production stages;
- dedicated Producer/Project Manager coordinates timelines, milestones, budget, resources, performance and vendors.

References:
- https://kurzgesagt.org/what-we-do?visit=videos
- https://kurzgesagt.org/careers/producer-all-genders-655515

### Veritasium
Recent public credits repeatedly separate:
- writers/directors;
- researchers;
- editors and assistant editors;
- animators;
- illustrators;
- thumbnail designers;
- production team;
- executive producers;
- and in some episodes sound designers.

Many episodes publish factual references separately from image/video references.

Adopted lesson:
claim evidence and visual-source provenance are separate production artifacts.

### Cleo Abram / Huge If True
Public interviews describe each highly produced episode as requiring several hundred hours of research and production over months.

Key process lesson:
the team asks what visual makes a story worth telling as video rather than text.

The key visual may be:
- a chart;
- a diagram;
- archival footage;
- a demonstration;
- custom animation.

That visual helps shape the story itself.

The animator/producer participates across pitching, scripting, production and review rather than entering only after script lock.

References:

Adopted lesson:

## What our current Council already does well

- Research and evidence are explicit.
- Editorial/showrunner authority is explicit.
- Script/voice direction is separate.
- Art direction is separate.
- Motion/picture editing is separate.
- Music/sound design and mix engineering are separated.
- Localization is a distinct function.
- Specialized-tool-first prevents code from replacing craft applications.
- Anti-slop and Owner Publish Gate are strong.
- Multilingual architecture is considered before production.

## Gaps versus professional studios

### 1. No dedicated Media Producer / Production Coordinator
Current Venture Operator is too broad.

Adopt:
Media Producer / Production Coordinator owns schedule, milestones, dependencies, resource plan, review calendar, vendor coordination, production risk and delivery readiness.

### 2. Fact checking is not independent enough
Research and factual clearance should not be the same function.

Adopt:
Independent Fact Checker.

Rule:
the function that researched or wrote a material claim cannot be the sole function that clears it.

### 3. Thumbnail craft is bundled into Growth
Growth strategy and visual thumbnail craft are different specialties.

Adopt:
split into:
- Audience Growth & Packaging Strategist;
- Thumbnail & Packaging Designer.

Growth owns audience hypothesis, title strategy, CTR learning and test design.

Thumbnail Designer owns composition, retouching, typography, visual hierarchy, brand consistency and test-ready variants.

### 4. Craft leads enter too late
Professional teams involve editor/animator/visual leads during story development.

Adopt:
Pre-production Craft Review before script lock.

Participants:
Showrunner, Producer, Research, Motion Editor, Art Director, Music/Sound, Packaging.

### 5. No explicit "why video?" proof
Adopt a Visual Story Case before packaging/script lock.

Required:
- key visual;
- key demonstration/diagram;
- archival/real-world evidence potential;
- one visual transformation/reveal;
- explicit answer to: why video, not article?

Fail:
return topic to backlog.

### 6. Script iteration is under-specified
Do not copy a fixed "12 drafts" quota.

Adopt five purposeful passes:
S0 Story Skeleton
S1 Evidence Draft
S2 Visual Draft
S3 Voice/Performance Draft
S4 Locked Script

No single-pass script may enter production.

### 7. Claim sources and visual sources should be separate
Adopt:
- Claim Source Sheet
- Visual Source / Rights Sheet

### 8. Editor must be a story partner
Motion & Picture Editor becomes required reviewer for:
- Visual Story Case;
- Story Skeleton;
- Script visual draft;
- transition feasibility;
- first-30-second pacing.

### 9. Production readiness needs a real gate
Adopt a Production Readiness Gate before expensive production.

Must confirm:
- script locked;
- key visuals approved;
- source rights clear;
- storyboard sufficient;
- audio spotting complete;
- asset list complete;
- tool capability available;
- schedule/budget acceptable.

### 10. Packaging needs two moments
Stage A before script:
story promise + title/thumbnail hypothesis.

Stage B after rough cut:
final title/thumbnail variants + A/B test plan.

## Adopted Media Studio Model

The Council keeps governance separate from the Media Production Cell.

The Media Production Cell becomes:
- Media Producer / Production Coordinator
- Editorial Director / Showrunner
- Evidence & Research Lead
- Independent Fact Checker
- Script & Voice Director
- Visual Art Director / Frame Curator
- Motion & Picture Editor
- Music Supervisor & Sound Designer
- Dialogue & Mix Engineer
- Audience Growth & Packaging Strategist
- Thumbnail & Packaging Designer
- Localization Producer (on demand)

These are functions, not mandatory headcount.

One person/tool may cover more than one function during the pilot, but critical review boundaries remain independent.

## New pre-production core

Before Script Lock:
Showrunner + Producer + Research + Motion + Art + Sound + Packaging answer:

1. What is the story question?
2. What is the answer?
3. Why is this a video?
4. What is the key visual?
5. What is the emotional/sonic arc?
6. What makes the first 30 seconds work?
7. What evidence proves the core claim?
8. What is expensive or risky?
9. What should not be produced?
10. Does the story still fit the channel identity?

## Revised script development

### S0 — Story Skeleton
hook → context → escalation → reveal → consequence → ending.

### S1 — Evidence Draft
Every factual claim receives an evidence ID.

### S2 — Visual Draft
Every major paragraph receives a visual beat, evidence/diagram/asset concept and editor feasibility note.

### S3 — Voice Draft
Narration rhythm, emotional tags, pronunciation, pauses and Owner performance.

### S4 — Locked Script
Fact Checker clears material claims.
Showrunner locks story.
Producer records version and downstream change impact.

## What is intentionally NOT copied

- Kurzgesagt-scale headcount.
- large permanent staffing.
- expensive 3D by default.
- fixed cadence before pilot evidence.
- a fixed draft count beyond the five purposeful passes.
- approval bureaucracy for its own sake.

We copy production disciplines, not organizational scale.

## Pilot measurements added

- schedule variance;
- milestone slips;
- script revision count;
- fact-check defects caught before lock;
- Visual Story Case rejection/rework;
- thumbnail variants produced;
- production readiness failures;
- edit rework after script lock.

After three videos:
HR + Producer + Finance review which functions can be safely combined.

## Final verdict

ADOPT.

The previous media architecture was strong in governance but still too AI-agent/software-workflow shaped.

The benchmark moves it toward a real editorial production studio:
- producer-led execution;
- visual-first story development;
- independent fact check;
- specialist packaging;
- editor involvement before script lock;
- iterative scripts;
- separate claim and visual-source sheets;
- professional production readiness before expensive execution.
