# Universal Council — Channel Operating System ARENA
Date: 2026-10-12
Status: FINAL OPERATING-MODEL DELIBERATION
Channel model: Broad format-first multilingual visual documentary
Visual identity: Editorial Renaissance Motion

## 1. Question

Build a complete operating method for the channel from trend detection through research, script, detailed voice direction, reference frames, transitions, production, multilingual delivery, QC, publication gating and post-publish learning.

The system must maximize:
- topic option value;
- visual coherence;
- narrative quality;
- originality;
- multilingual portability;
- production repeatability;
- low AI-slop risk.

It must minimize:
- generic AI visual sequences;
- repetitive templates;
- weak trend-chasing;
- unsourced claims;
- inconsistent voice;
- random transitions;
- duplicated production work between languages;
- unnecessary Council/tool overhead.

## 2. Fresh operating evidence

Current public tools/policies relevant to the workflow:
- Google Trends Trending Now provides current search-velocity signals.
- TikTok Creative Center exposes public trending hashtags/videos and analytics.
- YouTube is deprecating the Inspiration tab and directs creators toward Ask Studio for idea generation, audience insights and performance analysis.
- YouTube retention analytics explicitly highlights the first 30 seconds, top moments, spikes and dips.
- YouTube monetization policy rejects mass-produced, generic, repetitive or interchangeable content, including generic AI templates.
- YouTube requires disclosure when realistic synthetic/altered content could mislead viewers about real people/events/places.

Operational implication:
Trend discovery can be partially automated, but editorial selection, evidence verification, narrative originality and final quality cannot be delegated to a single generative pass.

## 3. Council independent positions

### Chair / Decision Controller
SUPPORT.
The channel needs a governed operating system rather than ad-hoc creator behavior.
Key condition: gates must stop bad work early; do not use a heavy Council round for every micro-decision.

### Evidence & Research Lead
STRONG SUPPORT.
Trend velocity is not evidence. Every selected episode needs a claim ledger and source pack before script lock.
Required distinction: FACT / INFERENCE / SPECULATION.

### Red Team
SUPPORT WITH ANTI-SLOP GATE.
The biggest failure mode is not factual error; it is visually polished sameness.
A recognizable style is good. Interchangeable substance is not.
At least three episode-specific motifs must exist before animation starts.

### Practicality & Execution
SUPPORT.
Use deterministic templates for documents, file structure and checks; keep creativity at thesis, writing, visual treatment and transitions.
Avoid asking the model to reinvent folder structures or QC criteria every episode.

### Risk & Assurance
SUPPORT.
Require copyright/source provenance, AI-disclosure review, sensitive-domain review and a publish gate.
Never allow a trend alone to justify health/legal/investment claims.

### Human / User Advocate
STRONG SUPPORT.
The owner's human voice is a core identity. Scripts must include detailed performance direction without turning narration into theatrical overacting.
Natural pauses and Iraqi+MSA warmth are preferred over anchor-style delivery.

### Cost-Benefit / Finance
SUPPORT.
Track research hours, visual generation credits, editing hours, owner minutes and revisions. A beautiful workflow that costs too much per minute is not scalable.

### Editorial Director / Showrunner
STRONG SUPPORT.
Packaging must begin before scripting. If the team cannot state the one-sentence promise and thumbnail idea, the story is not ready.
The script is written for scenes, not for a voiceover pasted on visuals.

### AI Visual & Post-Production Producer
STRONG SUPPORT.
Create a finite visual grammar and frame library, but never a rigid episode template.
Transitions should be motivated by object/shape/cause/time, not chosen from a preset pack after the fact.

### Audience Growth & Packaging
STRONG SUPPORT.
Build a daily trend radar and a weekly shortlist. Use cross-platform recurrence and "why now" as signals, but preserve evergreen reframing.
After launch, first-party channel data outranks generic trend data.

### Technical Operator
SUPPORT.
Create machine-readable episode manifests, asset registries and IDs so GUI/DC automation can later assemble deterministic parts without losing traceability.

### HR & Capability Director
SUPPORT.
No permanent new Council seat is required.
Use temporary functions: Trend Scout, Script Director, Frame Curator, Continuity Checker. Escalate to new tooling only when pilot metrics expose a real bottleneck.

## 5. Trend strategy

Do not ask "what is trending?" and then make that video.

Use a Signal → Question → Evergreen Reframe pipeline.

Example:
Signal: undersea cable outage trends.
Bad topic: "Latest undersea cable outage news."
Good topic: "What actually happens when one undersea internet cable breaks?"

Trend sources:
- Google Trends;
- TikTok Creative Center;
- YouTube Ask Studio and later channel analytics/comments;
- YouTube search/autocomplete/competitor outliers;
- Reddit/specialist communities;
- public news/science/technology/business feeds;
- Instagram/Threads/X public recurrence or Metricool when connected;
- first-party YouTube search terms, retention, comments and returning-viewer data after launch.

A trend candidate expires after 72 hours unless converted into a durable question.

## 6. Topic score

100-point model:
- Cross-platform velocity — 15
- Curiosity gap — 15
- Format fit — 12
- Evergreen half-life — 10
- Visual explainability — 12
- Evidence sourceability — 10
- Multilingual portability — 8
- Competition opportunity — 6
- Production cost efficiency — 5
- Policy/brand safety — 7

Hard gates:
- total >= 75;
- evidence sourceability >= 6/10;
- visual explainability >= 7/10;
- one-sentence question exists;
- trend can be reframed beyond a short news cycle.

## 7. Packaging-before-script rule

Before full script:
- 5 title hypotheses;
- 3 thumbnail concepts;
- one-sentence click promise;
- one-sentence answer promise;
- one "why now";
- one "why should this channel tell it?".

If truthful packaging cannot be produced, topic returns to the backlog.

## 8. Evidence Pack

Required before script lock:
- Claim ID;
- claim text;
- source;
- source date;
- primary/secondary;
- freshness;
- confidence;
- whether visual evidence exists;
- license/provenance;
- FACT / INFERENCE / SPECULATION label.

A financial/scientific/historical number without an evidence ID cannot enter the locked script.

## 9. Script and voice direction

The script is scene-aware.

Each narration unit contains:
- timecode;
- narration text;
- intent;
- emotion tag;
- energy 1–5;
- pace;
- pitch contour;
- emphasized words;
- pause before/after in milliseconds;
- breath note;
- pronunciation;
- visual beat ID;
- music state;
- SFX;
- evidence IDs;
- translation/localization note.

Approved recurring emotion tags:
- فضول هادئ
- سؤال حقيقي
- سر صغير
- دهشة مضبوطة
- ابتسامة خفيفة
- جدية
- تأمل
- تأكيد
- تحذير هادئ
- همس خفيف
- تسارع
- خاتمة مفتوحة

Voice principles:
- warm conversational Iraqi + MSA;
- no news-anchor cadence;
- no constant hype;
- no identical rhythm in every sentence;
- one or two emphasized words per sentence by default;
- pauses follow meaning, not a metronome;
- emotion direction describes intent, not acting technique.

## 10. Frame Bible

The channel uses 12 canonical frame families:
F01 Hero Question
F02 Evidence Document
F03 Editorial Portrait
F04 Scientific Engraving
F05 Mechanism Diagram
F06 Number/Scale Reveal
F07 Map/Spatial
F08 Object Macro
F09 Historical Collage
F10 Modern Reality
F11 Breathing Reset
F12 Ending Reflection

These are composition families, not reusable episode screenshots.

Every episode creates a Reference Frame Board before animation:
- minimum 8;
- target 12;
- maximum 18.

Each reference frame records:
- frame type;
- dominant object;
- palette subset;
- grid;
- negative-space percentage;
- text hierarchy;
- source/provenance;
- animation entry;
- animation exit;
- transition target;
- language-safe status.

## 11. Visual consistency without AI slop

Consistency comes from:
- palette;
- typography;
- grid;
- texture;
- shot taxonomy;
- motion grammar;
- voice identity;
- evidence treatment.

Variation comes from:
- episode-specific hero objects;
- unique diagrams;
- source documents;
- visual metaphors;
- transition motifs;
- historical/scientific imagery.

Mandatory rule:
No generated shot may be kept merely because it looks attractive.

Every shot must be tagged:
EVIDENCE / EXPLANATION / ATMOSPHERE / TRANSITION.

Atmosphere cannot dominate runtime.

Each episode must define at least three unique visual motifs not reused as motifs in the preceding episode.

## 12. Transition system

Transitions are planned during storyboard, not patched after editing.

A transition must have at least one motivation:
- same object;
- matching shape;
- matching color;
- camera direction;
- time change;
- causal relationship;
- scale relationship;
- semantic contrast.

Canonical transition grammar:
- oversized object wipe;
- mask reveal;
- shape match cut;
- color match cut;
- illustration → photograph;
- photograph → diagram extraction;
- zoom through object;
- editorial page shift;
- line-drawing build;
- parallax drift;
- foreground occlusion;
- cobalt reset frame.

Forbidden default behavior:
random preset wipe, repeated whip pan, meaningless glitch, hard-cut chains with no rhythm.

## 13. Asset Registry

Every asset receives:
- asset_id;
- episode_id;
- type;
- source;
- license/provenance;
- AI_generated true/false;
- realistic_synthetic true/false;
- disclosure_required;
- language_dependency;
- palette status;
- frame usage;
- modification history;
- final file path.

No unregistered asset enters final timeline.

## 14. Anti-Slop Gate

Before final polish, Red Team + Editorial + Visual Producer inspect:
- unique thesis;
- unique story structure;
- no interchangeable script;
- no repeated AI template sequence;
- no filler AI B-roll;
- every shot has a purpose tag;
- atmosphere is subordinate;
- source-linked claims;
- owner voice identity;
- palette/type/grid continuity;
- three episode-specific motifs;
- narratively motivated transitions.

Fail = return to Editorial/Visual Treatment, not "fix it with more effects."

## 15. Multilingual design

Arabic remains master narration.

Language-safe production:
- no avoidable baked-in Arabic text;
- editable AE/Premiere typography;
- 30–40% text expansion space;
- language-neutral diagrams where possible;
- one visual master;
- separate audio/subtitle/title/thumbnail manifests.

English localization begins only for winning pilots.
Spanish/German/French require language-level evidence.

## 17. Analytics loop

After publication:
- impressions;
- CTR;
- first 30 seconds retention;
- average percentage viewed;
- watch time;
- returning viewers;
- subscribers/video;
- top moments;
- spikes;
- dips;
- comment themes;
- language audio share.

YouTube's retention report specifically recommends reviewing the first 30 seconds and moving compelling later moments earlier when appropriate.

Postmortem answers:
- what promise earned the click?
- where did viewers leave?
- what was rewatched?
- what visual motif helped comprehension?
- what cost too much?
- what can graduate to a deterministic routine?
- what must never be templatized?
